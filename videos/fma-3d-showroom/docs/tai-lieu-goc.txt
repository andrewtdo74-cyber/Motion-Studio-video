Built and scanned in Vietnam. Tailored to your market. Ready to share with your customers.
fMA – furnitureMarket.Asia works with furniture manufacturers in Vietnam to develop bespoke 3D showrooms for international buyers, importers, distributors, dealers and other sales partners.
Partners can select the products, design style, layout and showroom size that suit their customers and target markets. Each physical display is staged in Vietnam, scanned into an interactive 3D showroom, and delivered via a shareable link for marketing, product presentations, and sales support.
Partners do not need to build an equivalent physical showroom in their own market or purchase the entire display collection upfront. Display products may be borrowed, rented or supplied under other support arrangements, subject to agreement with the manufacturers concerned.

An example: Three dealers in France, three distinctive showrooms
A furniture importer in France works with dealers in three cities. Each dealer serves customers with different preferences, living environments, and spending priorities.
The importer and its dealers can work with fMA and the relevant furniture manufacturers in Vietnam to develop three separate 3D showrooms, each reflecting the needs of its local customers.
Based on the dealers’ market knowledge and proposed direction, these might include:
Showroom One: Modern, compact furniture presented for customers living in urban apartments.


Showroom Two: Warm interiors featuring wood and natural colors, designed for customers who prefer a relaxed, welcoming style.


Showroom Three: Distinctive collections in more sophisticated settings, aimed at a premium customer segment.
These are illustrative concepts. Actual products and styles would be selected according to the importer’s and dealers’ understanding of their markets and the manufacturers’ ability to supply.
Staged in Vietnam. Used by each local sales partner.
The spaces are staged and scanned in Vietnam in accordance with the approved designs. Each dealer then receives its corresponding showroom link to share through its own channels, support customer consultations, and receive sales inquiries.

Expand your market presence without replicating the full cost of physical showrooms

The opportunity extends beyond a single 3D showroom: it is a flexible network of locally tailored presentations, supported by market feedback and connected to manufacturing capabilities in Vietnam.

5. HOW THE PROCESS WORKS
Step 1 - Define the market and business objectives
fMA, the manufacturer and the commercial partner identify the target customers, locations, product direction and intended use of the 3D showroom.
Step 2 - Agree on the design and project scope
The parties select the products, floor area, style, colors, finishes, room settings and branding requirements. They also agree on the budget, schedule, responsibilities and showroom usage terms.
Step 3 - Arrange display products and stage the space in Vietnam
fMA coordinates with the relevant manufacturers to arrange the location, display products and staging according to the approved plan.
Products may be borrowed, rented or provided under an agreed support program. Applicable fees, contributions or preferential arrangements are confirmed before implementation.
Step 4 - Scan and prepare the digital 3D showroom
The completed space is scanned to create the digital 3D showroom. Product details, branding and contact information are incorporated within the agreed content scope and available platform features.
Step 5 - Deliver the showroom link for marketing and sales support
The partner receives the showroom link and usage guidance to introduce the collection, support customer consultations, and receive purchasing inquiries in its market.
Step 6 - Return display products and dismantle the temporary space
After the digital showroom has been checked and accepted, display products are returned or handled as agreed, and the temporary physical display is dismantled.
The digital showroom remains accessible for the agreed service period.
Step 7 - Develop inquiries into confirmed orders
When purchasing requirements arise, the partner works with fMA and the relevant manufacturer to confirm product models, specifications, quantities, prices, production schedules and delivery arrangements.
Orders proceed under any applicable framework agreement and the specific sales contract or order confirmation between the responsible parties.
Step 8 - Develop subsequent collections and showroom versions
Based on customer feedback and business results, the parties may develop new collections, additional spaces or further showroom versions.
Changes to physical products or layouts may require restaging and rescanning, with the scope and cost agreed separately.

6. FLEXIBLE COOPERATION WITH CLEAR RESPONSIBILITIES
Depending on the project, costs may be covered by the manufacturer, the commercial partner, or shared between the parties. Preferential arrangements may be considered according to the project scale and specific cooperation commitments.
Before implementation, the parties agree on the following principal matters:
Scope and costs: Design, premises, sample transportation, staging, 3D scanning, sample returns and dismantling.


Display product management: Product lists, condition records, usage periods and responsibility for damage or loss.


Digital showroom operation: Hosting period, usage rights, sharing channels, maintenance and renewal terms.


Branding and commercial information: Approved content, image usage rights and confidentiality.


Customer inquiry handling: Contact points, inquiry routing and responsibility for following up sales opportunities.


Supply arrangements: Quotation procedures, minimum order quantities, payment, delivery, quality requirements and warranties.


Market coverage: Any distribution rights or exclusivity must be expressly agreed separately.
The 3D showroom is a product presentation and sales support tool. Availability, specifications, prices and delivery schedules must be confirmed when an order is placed. Creating a showroom does not, by itself, establish an obligation to purchase the displayed products unless separately agreed.
